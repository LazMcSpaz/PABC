`takeAITurn(state)` takes **no player argument**; it plays whoever
`activePlayerId(state)` returns and ends the turn itself. Passing a faction
id does nothing, which is how one fixture ended up playing the *human's*
seat with the AI. Drive a specific faction the way the AI smoke test does:
`if (g.players[activePlayerId(g)].isAI) takeAITurn(g); else endTurn(g)` —
and stop once `g.winnerId` is set, because after that `takeAITurn` no-ops
and a wait-for-it loop spins forever.

them at `src/prototype/engineAdapter.js`. The prototype layer never touches
engine internals directly.

## What shipped this session

**Quick wins (10)** — Oswald display font, board frame brackets, "scrap" not
"resource", quieter round-1 event feed, camera centred on your capital, real
Tech Wheel node names, real unit names, formatters for 58 unformatted event
types, fixed `npm run shots`, roadmap brought current.

**Diplomacy tiers 1 and 2** — Make Peace is refusable; Denounce costs Honor
and has a cooldown; one deal-item schema with struck deals actually forming
what they promise; Demand Tribute delivers the amount set; flows carry a
duration and expire; pending offers with expiry; counter-offers that return
the nearest deal they would take; a Standing cost to over-asking.

**Warranted denouncement** — `denounceWarrant()`. Denouncing a tyrant now
*gains* Honor; denouncing without grounds costs it, and the accusation is
judged for credibility *before* the Honor hit lands (that ordering was a real
bug — a baseless denouncement used to silence itself).

**The five depth layers** — grievance ledger you can be paid to settle;
reputation gated on what the board actually witnessed (fog of war now feeds
Menace via `witnessShare`); receipts showing the player how they got here;
claims; ultimatums (`issueUltimatum` / `answerUltimatum`, with AI
follow-through and a per-pair cooldown so the AI stops bluffing itself).

**Envoy audiences** — Civ-style dialogue boxes. Offers and ultimatums used to
sit silently in the drawer; now somebody comes to say them and takes an
answer. Two portrait registers in `src/prototype/factionPortraits.js`:
`LEADER_PORTRAITS` for the irreversible moments (war, peace, pacts,
coalitions, vassalage, recognition, elimination — see `toneForEvent()`) and
`ENVOY_PORTRAITS` for everything routine. All four faction envoys are in.
`EnvoyModal.jsx` is a shared `Audience` shell with three faces — warning,
offer, demand — and temperament-keyed flavour text.

**§3 second half — Locations as deal items (2026-08-20).** The last of the
five, and the one that moves diplomacy from a side-market into the same
currency as the war. `{ location: { hexId } }` is a deal item like any other:
it prices itself from the valuer's side (victory points + output + walls,
multiplied if they call the place home), costs more to give up than to gain
(scaled by aggression — a warlord does not sell land), and transfers
atomically with the rest of the deal. So *"cede Omara and we have peace"* is
a sentence the game can say, and the AI says it in both directions: naming
the price when it is winning, handing the city back when it is losing.

A city given is a city intact — deliberately not `captureLocation`, so
nothing is sacked and no chip is destroyed, though Loyalty still starts low
and the half-built workshop still resets. Not for sale: a city you only half
hold, a Capital, or the last ground you stand on. A cession the giver cannot
perform is refused with the reason rather than countered with scrap, and
re-checked when a tabled offer is finally *answered*, since a city can be
lost while an offer sits on the table for rounds. Ground is barred from
`demand-tribute` outright: `caveOnDemand` weighs a demand on the power ratio
alone, which is right for scrap and absurd for a city.

Two bugs surfaced on the way and are fixed: a faction losing a war would
**court a stranger with a gift instead of ending the war** (the gift branch
returns on every turn a sociable faction takes, so everything below it was
unreachable — the same obstacle the settle branch was hoisted over in §1),
and the **envoy audience box dropped every offer's `note`**, so an envoy
arrived with terms and no argument. 40 harness checks (Phase 30).
